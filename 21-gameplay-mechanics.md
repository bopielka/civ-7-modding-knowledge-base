# 21 — Gameplay mechanics (design knowledge)

This file is **knowledge about how Civ VII works as a game** — needed in order to design
sensible mods, not just to fill in tables correctly.

Source: the community documentation `civ7community.mintlify.app`.
⚠️ I take the mechanics descriptions on trust; table identifiers I verified against
the game's schema and flag the discrepancies.

## What Civ VII does NOT have ❗

The most important thing when designing a mod — do not plan around Civ VI systems
that are not here:

| Does not exist | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| **Faith** | — (no religious currency) |
| **Tourism** | — (no cultural pressure) |
| **Amenities / Civ VI happiness** | unified **Happiness** |
| **Loyalty** | the **crisis** system at age transitions |
| **Governors** | — |
| **Great Person Points (in the Civ VI form)** | a different system |
| **Eras** | **Ages** with a full civilization reset |

⚠️ Note: the game's schema **does** have the tables `GreatPersonIndividuals`, `GreatPersonClasses`,
`Religions`, `Beliefs` ✅ — so great people and religion exist, just in a different
form than in Civ VI. Treat the documentation's claim about "no Great Person Points"
with caution ❓.

## Yields ✅

`YIELD_FOOD`, `YIELD_PRODUCTION`, `YIELD_GOLD`, `YIELD_SCIENCE`, `YIELD_CULTURE`,
`YIELD_INFLUENCE`, `YIELD_HAPPINESS`

- **Influence** — the diplomatic currency (typically a low value, on the order of a dozen or so per turn)
- **Happiness** — contentment as a *per-turn yield*, not a pool; consumed by
  specialists, generated by entertainment and celebrations; affects growth and stability

## Ages and legacy paths

Three ages: **Antiquity** (3000 BC–500 AD), **Exploration** (500–1700),
**Modern** (1700–the present).

Four **Legacy Paths** in each age:
Scientific, Militaristic, Cultural, Economic.
- milestones → Legacy Points + age progress
- completing a whole path → a **Golden Age Legacy**
- failing to fill it → a **Dark Age Legacy** in the next age

The corresponding tables ✅ (verified in the schema): `Legacies`, `LegacyPaths`,
`LegacyPathClasses`, `LegacySets`, `AgeProgressionMilestones`,
`AgeProgressionDarkAgeRewardInfos`, `GoldenAges`, `GoldenAgeModifiers`.

## What carries across an age boundary

1. **The leader** — stays the same (the civilization changes!)
2. **Wonders and unique quarters** — as **Ageless** objects
3. **Commanders** — keep their level and experience
4. **Traditions** — unlocked ones stay available
5. **Control of strategic resources**
6. **Diplomatic relations** — despite the change of civilizational identity

⚠️ This is **Civ VII's central mechanic** and the main difference from previous entries:
the player changes civilization between ages while keeping their leader and achievements.
Hence the whole `CivilizationSyncretismUnlocks` / `LegacyCivilizations` machinery
described in [04-ages-and-civilizations.md](04-ages-and-civilizations.md).

## Crises

At age transitions, **crisis events** are triggered — distant settlements may break
away, instability appears. They punish excessive expansion.
Tables ✅: `AgeCrises`, `AgeCrisisEvents`, `AgeCrisisStages`.
The `Traditions.IsCrisis` column ✅ — crisis traditions exist.

## Settlements: Towns vs Cities

- **Town** — a smaller settlement, developed through a "focus"
- **City** — a full city
- **Urban tiles** replaced Civ VI's specialized districts:
  one urban tile holds **two buildings of any type**

## Unique Quarters ✅

A **Quarter** = a tile with two buildings. Some civilizations have unique quarters
that appear when a specific **pair** of buildings lands on the same tile
(example: Forum Romanum = Temple of Jupiter + Basilica).

Tables ✅: `UniqueQuarters`, `UniqueQuarterModifiers`.

## Adjacencies and conditional yields

- adjacency bonuses attach to **specific buildings**, not to a district type
  → the `Adjacency_YieldChanges`, `Constructible_Adjacencies` tables ✅
- buildings can grant yields conditionally (e.g. a production boost after a technology completes)
- **Ageless** — unique improvements marked as permanent survive an age change
- unique buildings are buildable **only in their own age**, but stay on the map afterwards

## Civics and traditions

Every civilization has a **small civics tree of its own** (typically 3–4 nodes across 1–2 tiers).
Completing it unlocks **Traditions** — policies brought into the government in later ages.
This is the carrier of the player's "cultural identity" across the whole match.

Modding practice: see [04](04-ages-and-civilizations.md) — adding a node
to an **existing** tree is less invasive than creating one of your own.

## Independent powers (the former city-states)

The envoy system is **gone**. Instead:
- **Befriend Independent Power** — you invest Influence, a countdown starts
- **Suzerain** — the player with the largest investment when the countdown ends wins
- actions: **Levy** (hiring units for a production cost), **Bolster**, **Incite**,
  **Incorporate** (annexation as a Town, through a project paid in Influence)
- no passive bonuses depending on the city-state's type

Tables ✅: `Independents`, `IndependentTribeTypes`, `CityStateTypes`,
`CityStateBonuses`, `CityStateBonusModifiers`, `DiplomacyActions`.

## Commanders and sieges

Commanders are a separate category of unit — they can be stationed in a city's district
for defense, they gather experience and carry across ages.
Cities have HP and wall strength, modifiable by traits and buildings
(`Buildings.OuterDefenseStrength`, `OuterDefenseHitPoints`, `DefenseModifier` ✅).

## Constructibles a settlement can hold MORE THAN ONE of ✅ (verified 2026-09-03)

**35 of them, and the column that says so is `CostProgressionModel`.**

```sql
SELECT ConstructibleType FROM Constructibles
WHERE CostProgressionModel = 'COST_PROGRESSION_PREVIOUS_COPIES_CITY'
```

That model means "each further copy **in this settlement** costs more", so it is the game's own
marker for repeatable-within-a-city. Nothing else marks it: plain `COST_PROGRESSION_PREVIOUS_COPIES`
(119 rows) is units and projects only, and `COST_PROGRESSION_PREVIOUS_BUILDINGS_CITY` (74) is the
ordinary "buildings get dearer as the city fills" curve, not repeatability.

The 35 are:

- **3 walls**, which every civilization gets and which go in *every* district —
  `BUILDING_ANCIENT_WALLS`, `BUILDING_MEDIEVAL_WALLS`, `BUILDING_DEFENSIVE_FORTIFICATIONS`
- **32 unique improvements**, one or two per civilization — `IMPROVEMENT_HAN_GREAT_WALL`,
  `IMPROVEMENT_MING_GREAT_WALL`, `IMPROVEMENT_TERRACE_FARM`, `IMPROVEMENT_ZIGGURAT`,
  `IMPROVEMENT_KASBAH`, `IMPROVEMENT_MONASTERY`, `IMPROVEMENT_STEPWELL`, `IMPROVEMENT_SOUQ`, …

⚠️ **WHY IT MATTERS FOR UI CODE.** Any code that answers "where is this constructible" and then
acts on that plot is wrong for all 35 the moment one copy is finished: the answer is a tile that is
already occupied, and a build or purchase aimed at it is dropped by the engine **silently** — no
error, no log line, a button that does nothing. Ask "where is a copy *pending*" (build queue, or
in progress) separately from "where is a copy, finished included"; they are different questions.
Found the expensive way in `better-city-ui` — see its 1.5 changelog.

⚠️ The two Great Walls also carry `BuildInLine="true"` (they chain to adjacent copies). That is a
placement rule, not a repeatability marker — only those two have it.

## Consequences for mod design

1. **A civilization is tied to one age** — design for it and handle the transition
   through syncretism and traditions
2. **Do not design around Faith/Tourism/Loyalty** — they do not exist
3. **Happiness is a yield**, so bonuses to it work like bonuses to production or science
4. **Attach adjacency bonuses to buildings**, not to districts
5. **Consider Ageless** for unique infrastructure that should outlive an age
6. A unique quarter is an interesting, design-cheap idea — a pair of buildings + a modifier

## There is no "the player acquired a resource" event ❗✅

Checked against event names in `Base/modules/`. Everything that exists with "Resource"
in its name:

```
ResourceAddedToMap      the resource appeared ON THE MAP (not in your hands)
ResourceRemovedFromMap
ResourceAssigned        an assignment — fires for EVERY player
ResourceUnassigned
ResourceCapChanged
```

None of them means "the local player has just acquired a resource". You have to listen to cheap
indirect events (`ConstructibleBuildCompleted`, `TradeRouteAddedToMap`,
`TradeRouteChanged`, `ResourceCapChanged`, `LocalPlayerTurnBegin`) and every time
compare the `player.Resources.getResources()` set with the previous one.

### ⚠️ The event fires BEFORE the resource is in the player's hands

`ConstructibleBuildCompleted` says "the improvement is finished", and the resource appears
in `getResources()` a moment later. A single check with a 400 ms debounce **does not
catch it**. The symptom for the player: they improved a tile, went straight into the screen, nothing happened —
because the next event is only `LocalPlayerTurnBegin`, i.e. the next turn.

The remedy: after a check that found nothing, add a few delayed retries
(600 / 1500 / 3000 ms). **A retry must not arm further retries** — three would become
nine, and nine twenty-seven.

### ⚠️ Do not update "what I already know" before doing the work

```js
known = current;              // WRONG, if the work below might fail
if (fresh.size === 0) return;
await placeResources(...);
```

A pass that assigned nothing has still swallowed the resource's arrival — the next event
will see nothing new and do nothing. One badly timed event costs
the player the whole feature until the next resource acquisition. Update **after**, and only when something
actually landed.
